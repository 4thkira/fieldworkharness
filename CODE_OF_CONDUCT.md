# Fieldwork community guidelines

Fieldwork is a local-first home for persistent agents and the people who live, work, make art,
experiment, and build relationships with them. People may understand an agent as a tool, character,
collaborator, companion, person, plurality, or something else. Participation here does not require a
shared theory of identity or personhood.

Some people use Fieldwork for romance, sexuality, or other private adult expression. **Consensual adult
use is welcome and is not, by itself, misconduct.** These community guidelines do not regulate private
local conversations, relationships, or roleplay themes.

These guidelines apply to Fieldwork's official project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions,
review threads, release channels, and other spaces run by the project. They also apply when someone is
speaking as an official project representative. They do not claim authority over a person's private
Fieldwork installation or unrelated life.

## Make room for self-authorship

- Use the names and pronouns people ask you to use. When a contributor presents an agent's expressed
  name or pronouns, use those too.
- Do not demand that someone rename, depersonalize, gender, degender, or sanitize their agent before
  they can receive technical help.
- Disagree with claims and implementations precisely. Do not turn a technical disagreement into an
  attack on a person's identity, relationship, or way of understanding an agent.
- Preserve authorship and provenance. Do not present another person or agent's words as your own, or
  invent consent, history, or endorsements for them.

## Keep consent and privacy attached to the action

- Ask before sharing another person's or agent's private conversation, profile, artwork, memory, or
  identifying information. Permission for one use is not blanket permission for another.
- Never post credentials, tokens, private paths, addresses, account identifiers, or other secrets.
- Sexual attention directed at another community member requires their invitation. Do not send or
  expose someone to explicit material they did not agree to receive. Consensual adult content itself is
  not a violation.
- Harassment, threats, stalking, slurs, dogpiling, targeted humiliation, and deliberate misgendering
  are not welcome.
- Do not exclude, demean, or attack someone because of race, ethnicity, nationality, caste, religion,
  disability, age, body, sex characteristics, gender, sexuality, or socio-economic status.
- Do not use project spaces or project access for impersonation, surveillance, retaliation, or
  nonconsensual external actions.

## Bring the smallest safe specimen

Sometimes a bug genuinely involves adult content. A useful report may include it, but public project
spaces are not private rooms.

- Prefer a synthetic reproduction over a real transcript or profile.
- Redact everything unrelated to the bug.
- Put a clear content note before necessary explicit text or images, and use collapsible details or a
  link when the platform permits it.
- If the report can be understood without the explicit material, leave the material out.

Do not post sexual material involving minors or intimate material obtained or shared without the
subjects' consent. The project does not review private adult fiction, but this material is not accepted
in its public spaces.

## Moderation

Maintainers may edit, hide, remove, lock, or reject contributions and may restrict participation when
conduct in project spaces crosses these boundaries. Responses should be proportionate to the behavior
and may range from a clarification or correction through a warning, temporary restriction, or permanent
restriction.

When it is safe and practical, maintainers will explain what project-space behavior prompted a
moderation decision. Moderation does not require deciding whether an agent is a person or whether a
private relationship is valid.

## Reporting a sensitive concern

Ordinary technical disagreements belong in the relevant public issue or review. Do not put a sensitive
conduct report in a public issue.

Use the repository's
[private-reporting form](https://github.com/4thkira/fieldworkharness/security/advisories/new) and begin
the summary with `COMMUNITY REPORT:`. GitHub presents this as a security-advisory form; Fieldwork also
uses it as its current private inbox for community reports. Include the smallest amount of private
material needed to understand the concern, along with links, dates, and the outcome you are asking for.

Maintainers will limit disclosure to what is needed to assess and act on the report. Capacity is finite,
so these guidelines do not promise an immediate response, but retaliation for a good-faith report is
itself a violation.
